rivverun

A Joycean prose experiment about identity, self-consciousness, and the feeling of being carried through life by forces that never quite feel like your own.

Riverrun, past Adam and Eve—the river being me or whatever—obviously, and the running through swerve and bend my motion, which is not quite me, but some other transient force, inbent and twisting me ’round like the deep eddies that killed me—well, maybe not me per se, but a part of me that, again, here I misspoke, didn’t die as much as got sucked under and shot out to sea, only to emerge briefly as a floating whitecap, bobbing and swaying in the deep tide, then realizing it lost its place and is but a fragment of a person’s past, a piece of an ever-emptying puzzle slowly being reduced to just a few pieces—not by intentional reduction but by a nebeneinander of building further complexity into one piece, etching and re-etching with microneedles, epidermal spikes, minuscule thoughts, time, love, effort, sweat, tears, and life itself, which is only possible through letting pieces fall away into the river along the way.

I’ve gotten a bit ahead of myself, so before I show you where and how and why the riverruns as it does, allow me to breathe. Allow me to notice each and every facet and faciale and rivulet—allow me to observe each part of my countenance: the protruding upper lip that is only made up for by a conscious sticking out of the lower one, eyebrows (at this point subconsciously) mastered to raise to veil the forehead’s immeasurable length, the curls of hair that I so carefully use to precisely cover the receding corners, my glazed eyes that hide any and all true emotion—wow! All this happening all at once—some of it happening of my own fruition, but others just working themselves through some sort of subconscious tic or taught tactic or even evolutionary mating instinct that makes the intrasex competition at least a little more interesting.

But notice! Notice what happens as I concentrate on something else—not just anything—but something ephemeral and fleeting, a figurant or freedom or flailing in the tide that overpowers me or milieu or any sort of rational reasoning or disposition. These facial facets fade away—first the eyes, then the lip, followed by the eyebrows, hair falling out of its set place without notice.

Ok. And it did so just now.

Breathe.

Dissipation.

Push. Pull. Suppress.

Take control.

With enough digression had, let’s actually let the riverrun where it runs, which happens to not have all too much variability, but alas still runs, and don’t forget what they say:

rivergoessomewhereventuallyevenifsomewheremeanssuddendeath.

What a silly flash of thought to spark through my head! Let’s open the door and breathe in the cold, rainy air. The petrichor replaces the mold dust and filament ever-present, never-seen in my underground sepulcher that is a realized resting and charging place. Sometimes when I lay in bed staring at the smoke-yellow ceiling, I feel a shovel or spade or scythe poking me just above my right navel vein. It’s almost as if someone is attempting to dig me out, excavate my soul. As the cold metal digs deeper, I start to see three faces—all men related but not too related—staring down at me with unfocused expressions of mild intrigue and dizzy quiescence, sick little grins forming on their faces.

When that image comes into full focus, in dreams or otherwise reality, I blink and come to. I snap upwards, perform my ablutions carefully, scrape the mud from the last time I went out off my shoes, and open the door to see a slowly setting sun and unbusy Sólgata with shuttered windows, hopefully on vacation and not just gone forever.

I think they are.

Anyway, I must continue my search for her. I was close last time—had to recede before she was found due to an exhaustion I can only relate to pushing all seventy-three buttons on an elevator, sitting in a corner, and cackling at those who dare to enter. Once seventy-three is one away, I get off at seventy-two and romp downwards, holding my breath and skipping every third stair.

Okay, that’s cute and all, but what about the mud? Where’d it come from?

Well, the cackling is too clean. When the laughing or crying or stepping or whatever is done with, and I exit the spinning glass doors—slamming face-first into the door trying to get on out of there so fast, the receptionist’s grin fading when she realizes someone’s gonna have to clean up the pulpy nose-mess spinning and fractaling around the funhouse windows, and waiting for the janitorial squad is pretty much an absolute no-no from her voice—I realize that nothing really felt like anything of value or substance anyway, and I finally let out my held breath and lose the structured countenance—probably looking at best about to cry and at worst psychotically insane—so I can look around for some sort of above-ground but still secluded sojourn to lay down and, laying prone, smell the grass for a second just to feel real and actually there, if only for a moment.

Which obviously gets pretty dirty and sticky and muddy if it has been raining all day and you live in an area like mine, where it is flat and dead and pretty much entrenched with endless peat bogs, lagoons, and lakes of leeches. You learn that it was probably a pretty silly thing to do, all that, but oh well, you live to (and you) learn, so viva la vida, carpe diem, or some other sickly sweet aphorism to invoke a whole feeling or vibe or way of stoic being so the reader or listener or whatever can nod their head in knowing agreement.

I’ve stalled long enough. Let’s find her now.

Even with the people gone, the day looks good—the mossy gray bricks are lined in perfect order, ready to be trodden over. The only sign of life is a calico-colored cat sifting through a cigarette-butt-infested garbage can that has a floppy banana peel hanging on to the side for dear life. The cat almost seems to grimace, but I know that really it’s just holding its face together because even cats care.

There’s something odd about garbage cans. Something not quite right. It’s a relatively innocuous receptacle with its black umbrella and four slotted openings, but it holds some profound truths about the human condition. It’s something we search for often but never touch or worry about what’s in it or where it goes.

Out of sight, out of mind, amiright?

Right?

Why don’t we worry about it?

Because it’s garbage.

Duh.

It holds the unwanted. The Dalit.

Yet we all seek it out from time to time. The flimsy piece of plastic in our hands, the shiny, overwrought and overmarketed aluminum can—both with the good stuff already in here, churnchurnchurning away, laundromat-like. I suppose that with time the good stuff turns bad too—the nutrients stolen, the messy mush and acidic foam suppurating from a shriveled organ, to be left behind in a differently shaped, oblong receptacle that shoots shit underground.

Time makes all things worse, I think.

The elevator ride, the effete puzzle pieces.

You get the gist.

And so why do we attract the unwanted just to get rid of it? Why are we magnets pulling good and bad alike in?

I think it’s because, to maintain the good, the status quo, we must have a ratio in our favor.

G > B.

Whenever the sign flips or equals out, it gets bad for us people.

Real bad.

The juice is not worth the squeeze.

Good thing we have these bad dispensers, right?

Right??

Good thing we have these easy-use, free-to-use containers that get replaced when they are full. What a fantastically fortunate ordeal!

But do we just do this with the inanimate? The assigned? For cans just fill, never consume.

Is there—and I say this with the utmost concern—anything or anyone that implicitly consumes the bad stuff? Are they complicit? Do they not open their mouth wide, tonsils and too-red throat in full view, and say pretty please?

Boy, I am stuck in this spot.

Right here at the doorstep.

If not for the sun’s fall to the horizon, I’d think I’m stuck in an eddy, swirling around like that oblong underground receptacle.

Is it too much to unstick my shoes, put up the guard, and go?

Can I not form an at least somewhat agreeable face for her? Pretend enough to suck her into the eddy I’m spinspinspinning ’round in, right now, at this very moment?

Just let my fraudulent fooling, my titillating teasing work, if only for one day.

Let me feel someone else’s love.

Let me feel how it feels to be on the receiving end of me.

Could I even tell?

Could she communicate?

I’ll go back inside for now.

Figure this puzzle out.

Find out the capital-T Truth.

Because if I said all or any of this to her, she’d give me a strange stare and purse her lips. She’d shift her feet this way and that, anywhere that’s not forward, not towards me.

She’d tell me it’s not that deep.

But it is!

She’s standing on the bank. How would she know?

She hasn’t felt the riverrun, the eddies swirl. She hasn’t had pieces of her get thrown around by swerve and bend and bay to become a whitecap floating on a moonlit ocean of rolling ripples.

She sits on the sandy bank and wanly smiles, dipping her feet in the water and nonchalantly playing with her hair.

She watches from afar.

So today’s not the day.

The eddy appeared too soon—at my doorstep—pulled a part of me away.

And so before going back inside, back home, I look at where the sun used to be and say:

riverruns, it moves in inbent spirals, sloshing sideways, sinking deeper, diving down.

For you the river just runs.

It’s just words or lives or time or feelings you see as play.

To me it’s inside.

It runs, the river does, from inside out, curving and churning, until it’s swallowed out at bay.
